# **CELL BIOLOGY, EMBRYOLOGY AND HISTOLOGY** 

# **Exam format:** 

The final exam will be a 90-minutes long written test made of 70 questions, on all the three modules, divided as follows: 

- Cell Biology 30 questions, 

- Histology 24 questions, 

- Embryology 12 questions 

- and 4 interdisciplinary questions. 

<u>Questions’ formats: A majority of multiple choice question intermixed with open</u> questions (ca 50-200 words), Fill in the gaps, True or False and Matching. 

<u>Minimal requirements for passing: Reaching an overall 60% of correct answers with</u> at least 50% of correct answers in each module. Additional points for Projects (where applicable) will be added to the score only if all the previous requirements have been met. 

# **MODULE OF CELL BIOLOGY** 

# **Topic 1. Structure and function of cells** 

**Content:** The cell as the fundamental unit of life. Prokaryotes and Eukaryotes. Cell membranes. The fluid-mosaic model of the cell plasma membrane. Main structural features and functions of membranes. Principles of transmembrane transport. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Illustrate the main differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells 

- Recognize and illustrate the cell membrane: structure, specialized functions, 

- mechanisms of transport 

# **Topic 2. Structure and function of cells (part 2)** 

**Content:** Cytoskeleton; Nucleus and nuclear envelope, the endoplasmic reticulum. **Learning goals:** 

- Illustrate the structure and function of microfilaments, intermediate filaments and 

- microtubules 

- Recognize and illustrate the morphology and function of the nucleus, chromatin 

- arrangement, nuclear envelope, nucleolus and nuclear pores 

- Recognize and illustrate the morphology and specific functions of the rough and 

- smooth endoplasmic reticulum 

# **Topic 3. The cellular cytoskeleton and the nucleus** 

**Content:** The Golgi apparatus, endocytosis, endosomes and lysosomes; peroxisomes and mitochondria 

**Learning goals:** 

- Recognize and illustrate the structure and function of the Golgi apparatus 

- Illustrate the process of endocytosis 

- Recognize endosome, lysosomes and peroxisomes 

- Illustrate the structure and functions of mitochondria 

- Clinical drop: Lysosomal storage diseases 

# **Topic 4. Protein structure** 

**Content:** the central dogma of molecular biology, protein classification, protein constituents, the peptide bond and its properties, the four levels of protein structure, protein function regulation 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Understand the flow of information within cells 

- Illustrate the main functions of proteins 

- Describe the classification of amino acids 

- Describe the four levels of protein structure 

# **Topic 5. Structural and functional properties of DNA molecules** 

**Content:** DNA as the genetic material. Structure of the genetic material, physicochemical properties of DNA molecules. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the structure of DNA 

- Discuss the structure-function relationships of DNA molecules 

# **Topic 6. Chromatin and chromosomes** 

**Content:** The organization of DNA in the cell nucleus, chromatin structure, histones, how chromatin features is inherited during cell division. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Illustrate how the genetic material is organized in the cell nucleus 

- Describe the levels of chromatin compaction 

# **Topic 7. The mechanism of DNA replication** 

**Content:** Semi-discontinuous replication, replication strategies, prokaryotic and eukaryotic DNA polymerases, priming. The problem of the replication of chromosome ends 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Discuss molecular mechanism of DNA replication 

- Discuss the problems raised by DNA replication and how the different proteins 

- participating in the process are able to solve them **Clinical drop** : Telomerase and cancer 

# **Topic 8. RNA transcription and metabolism: the transcriptome** 

**Content:** Eukaryotic transcription: Eukaryotic transcription factors - general & specific. Complexity of the transcriptome. 

**Learning goals:** 

- Discuss the flow of genetic information 

- Describe the mechanism of RNA synthesis (transcription) in prokaryotes and 

- eukaryotes 

# **Topic 9. RNA processing and gene expression regulation** 

**Content:** How mRNAs are processed in eukaryotes, what is RNA splicing. Gene expression regulation in prokaryotes. The RNA world. 

**Learning goals:** 

- Describe the RNA processing 

- Describe how gene expression is regulated in prokaryotes 

# **Topic 10. The genetic code and protein synthesis** 

**Content:** How can 4 nucleotides specify for 20 amino acids. The main properties of the genetic code. How mutations can affect the protein product of a nucleotide sequence. How protein synthesis takes place in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the properties of the genetic code 

- Describe the mechanism of protein synthesis and its regulation 

- Understand how mutations in DNA can affect protein sequences 

- Clinical drop: antibiotics, inhibitors of protein synthesis 

# **Topic 11. Protein folding** 

**Content:** How proteins acquire their final conformation and their functional properties after synthesis. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Understand how proteins acquire their final conformation and their functional 

- properties after synthesis 

- Understand the biological role of chaperone proteins 

- Describe pathological consequences of protein misfolding and/or aggregation 

- Illustrate the variety of post-translational modifications 

- Clinical drop: Alzheimer’s and Prion diseases 

# **Topics 12. Protein sorting and secretory pathway** 

**Content** : Fate of proteins after synthesis. Signals and mechanisms of protein sorting to organelles and secretory pathway. Vesicle trafficking and endocytosis. **Learning goals:** 

- Discuss general requirements for protein sorting 

- Discuss how proteins are guided to the nucleus, mitochondria, peroxisomes and 

- lysosomes 

- Describe the secretory pathway (exocytosis) and how proteins are delivered to 

- ER, Golgi, plasmamembrane and lysosomes 

- Illustrate the endocytic pathway 

- Describe the different pathways used to target protein to different cell 

- compartments 

- Describe mechanisms controlling proper protein folding during sorting from ER 

- Clinical drop: Unfolded protein response in human diseases 

# **Topics 13. Cell signaling** 

**Content:** General principles of cell signaling. Signal transduction. Nuclear receptors. G-protein coupled receptors. Enzyme-coupled receptors. Ion channel-coupled receptors. Second messengers. Protein kinases and molecular switches. **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the different types of intercellular communication 

- Define the main mechanisms of signal transduction by different types of receptors 

- Discuss the central role of protein kinases in regulating this process 

- Describe the main pathways of signal transduction by GPCRs and RTKs 

- Understand the role of second messengers 

# **Topic 14. Mitosis** 

**Content:** Overview on mechanisms of cell division. Chromosome behavior during cell division in somatic cells. Role of cytoskeleton during mitosis. 

**Learning goals:** 

- Describe the morphological aspect of nucleus during mitosis 

- Describe the stages of mitosis and explain the significance of each step 

- Describe the main cytoskeletal structures involved in cell division 

# **Topic 15. Cell cycle regulation** 

**Content:** Cell cycle phases. Regulation of cell cycle by extracellular stimuli. Cell cycle checkpoints. Role and regulation of cyclins. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Discuss cell cycle phases 

- Discuss how extracellular stimuli can regulate cell cycle 

- Discuss the significance of cell cycle checkpoints 

- Discuss the role of cyclins in regulating cell cycle 

# **Topic 16. Meiosis** 

**Content:** Chromosome behavior during cell division in germinal cells. Gametogenesis. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the function and the events that characterize meiotic division 

- Illustrate the mechanism of crossing-over 

- Understand how genetic variability is produced during meiosis 

- Discuss the differences between mitosis and meiosis 

# **Topic 17. How genes are inherited** 

**Content:** Mendel laws, The genetic significance of meiosis. Extensions to Mendel's laws. Penetrance, expressivity, pleiotropy. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe chromosome dynamics during meiosis, and their consequences at the 

- genetic level 

- Describe Mendel’s laws in the light of the meiosis process 

- Discuss the extensions/exceptions to Mendel’s laws 

- Describe penetrance, expressivity, and pleiotropy concepts 

- Discuss how genes and environment can interact in determining genetic traits in 

- humans 

# **Topic 18. Cytogenetics and clinical cytogenetics** 

**Content:** Preparation and interpretation of the karyotype. Genomic and chromosomal abnormalities and their importance in the phenotype and reproductive risk. Overview of the main techniques (classical karyotyping, FISH -Fluorescence in Situ Hybridization- and CGH - Comparative Genomic Hybridization-arrays). **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the main methods for chromosome analysis and their application to the 

- diagnosis of genetic disorders 

- Describe the human karyotype using the relative nomenclature 

- Describe the main numerical and structural chromosome anomalies 

- Discuss pro, cons, limits of the cytogenetic analysis 

# **Topic 19. Genetic variation** 

**Content:** Polymorphisms and mutations: classification and functional consequences. **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the different types of point mutations 

- Discuss differences between germinal and somatic mutations, and between 

- polymorphisms and mutations 

- Describe the functional consequences of the different types of mutation at the 

- RNA and protein levels 

# **Topics 20. Oncogenes and cancer** 

**Content:** General facts and stats about cancer. Cancer as a disease of cell behavior. Hallmarks of cancer. In vitro contact inhibition and in vivo tumor progression. Control of cell proliferation in cancer. Classes of oncogenes. Mechanisms of protooncogenes activation. Chromosomal alterations and cancer. Multiple mutations in cancer progression. Tumor suppressor genes. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Understand genetic and environmental contribution to carcinogenesis 

- Understand the altered properties of cancer cells and cancer heterogeneity 

- Discuss tumor progression by multiple mutations 

- Discuss the differences between oncogenes and tumor suppressors 

- Discuss molecular mechanisms of oncogenesis 

# **Topic 21. Apoptosis** 

**Content:** Differences between necrosis and apoptosis. Roles of apoptosis. Molecular regulation of apoptosis. Intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways. Caspases. The apoptosome. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe developmental, physiological and pathological processes in which 

- apoptosis plays a crucial role 

- Define molecular pathway(s) involved in apoptosis 

- Discuss regulator, adaptor and effector molecules in apoptosis 

- Discuss hallmarks of apoptotic cells 

# **Topic 22. Viruses** 

**Content:** Classification of viruses: DNA and RNA viruses. Structure of viral particles. Viral tropism. The cycle and the genome organization of retroviruses. Role in human disease. Use of viral vectors for gene therapy. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the structure of the main classes of animal viruses 

- Understand the differences between naked and enveloped viruses 

- Understand general principles guiding viral replication 

- Describe how retroviruses infect and replicate within cells 

- Clinical drop: the HIV vaccine 

# **Topic 23. Stem cells and disease modeling** 

**Content:** Properties and sources of stem cells. Induced pluripotent stem cells. Cell fate determination. Application of stem cells in therapy. Modelling diseases with iPSCs and organoids. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Understand the basic properties of stem cells, the concepts of potency, cell fate 

- determination, and reprogramming 

- Understand the differences between embryonic stem cells, adult stem cells and 

- induced-pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) 

- Describe the applications of stem cells and organoids in research and medicine 

- Clinical drop: epidemolysis bulbosa, limbal stem cell therapy, Parkinson’s disease 

# **Topic 24. Gene editing** 

**Content:** Genome editing tools. The CRISPR-Cas9 system in bacteria. CRISPR-Cas systems for genome editing. CRISPR-Cas technology in medicine. 

# **Learning goals:** 

- • Learn the systems that can be used to perform genome editing 

- Learn the basis of CRISPR-Cas9 technology 

- Discuss the application of genome editing in research and medicine

# **Module of general Embryology** 

During this module, the main events characterizing male and female gametogenesis, fertilization and implantation of the human embryo will be addressed. Following a description of the early stages of embryogenesis, some major topics in organogenesis will be discussed as to understand the main morphological events and the related molecular aspects characterizing the development of the body and its various organ systems. On this basis, the morphological and structural features of mature organs will be described stressing functional aspects. 

# **Topic 1. Principles of gametes production and maturation. Female gametogenesis and overview of the related changes in the female genital tract and hormonal control. Learning goals:** 

- Describe the main phases of gametogenesis (origin and migration of germ cells, increase in their number, reduction in chromosomal numbers, structural and functional maturation) 

- Describe the structural aspects of the ovary 

- Describe the main features of oogenesis and ovulation: the ovarian cycle 

- Outline the intrinsic and extrinsic hormonal guidance of the ovarian cycle 

- Illustrate the changes in the ovaries during the ageing process. 

- Outline the general structure of the uterus and uterine tube and of their cyclic changes 

# **Topic 2. Male gametogenesis and overview of the male genital system. Learning goals:** 

- Outline the components of the male genital system and the general structure of the testis and epididymis. 

- Describe the histological features of the seminiferous epithelium 

- Illustrate the relevance of the “blood-testis” barrier 

- Describe the process the leads from spermatogonia to spermatozoa: spermatogenesis and spermiogenesis 

- Understand the role of Sertoli cells 

- Describe the general aspect of the spermatogenic cycle and spermatogenic wave 

- Outline the intrinsic and extrinsic hormonal guidance of male spermatogenesis 

# **Topic 3. Fertilization. Early stages of the embryo development Learning goals:** 

- Describe the transport of the egg and sperm 

- Describe the process of fertilization 

- Describe the cleavage process of the zygote and the relevance of the different components of the early embryo: inner cell mass, trophoblast, zona pellucida, blastocele 

- Describe the embryo transport to the uterus 

- Describe the process of implantation into the uterine lining: formation of the trophoblastic plate and placental villi 

- Describe the transformation of the inner cell mass (stem cell link) into the embryonic shield (disc): becoming bilaminar 

- Describe the formation of the amniotic cavity, of the primary and secondary yolk sacs and of the extraembryonic mesoderm 

# **Topic 4: Gastrulation. Becoming trilaminar. Third week of Human Development** 

# **Learning goals:** 

- Describe the role of primitive streak and primitive node and the formation of the body axes 

- Describe the formation of the three primitive layers 

- Describe the notochord and its role. 

- Describe the formation of the neural plate and neural tube. 

- Describe the destiny of the neural crest 

- Clinical drop: Developmental defects: situs inversus and Kartagener syndrome; Developmental defects: sirenomielia 

# **Topic 5. The 4th week: establishing the body plan. Planning is everything Learning goals:** 

- Illustrate the concept of organogenesis 

- Describe the destiny of the three embryonic layers 

- Illustrate the concept of segmentation 

- Describe the outcome of the folding of the embryo: formation of the intestinal tube, formation of the body cavities, formation of the body wall 

- Describe the formation and general organization of the embryonic circulation 

- Explain some basic molecular aspects characterizing the establishment of the body plan 

- Relate the developmental aspects of the body plan to the adult body organization Clinical drops: Developmental defects - gastroschisis and omphalocele

# **Learning goals:**
