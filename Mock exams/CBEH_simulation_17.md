Model Answer: The end-replication problem arises because DNA polymerase can only synthesize in the 5' to 3' direction and requires an RNA primer; on the lagging strand, once the terminal RNA primer is removed, there is no upstream sequence to prime replacement DNA synthesis at the very end of the chromosome, causing progressive shortening of telomeres with each replication cycle. Telomerase is normally inactive in most somatic cells, leading to progressive telomere shortening and eventual replicative senescence (Hayflick limit). In contrast, most cancer cells reactivate telomerase, allowing unlimited replicative potential (immortalization), one of the hallmarks of cancer, by preventing telomere-induced senescence or crisis.
